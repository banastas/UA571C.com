# The idea

Yes, there is something here.

The useful connection to SemioticStandard.org is not merely the *Alien* universe or Ron Cobb-adjacent production design. It is the feeling that an artifact escaped the film and became usable. The Semiotic Standard turns a design sheet into a browsable object. This project turns a prop screen into a working object.

## The hook

The visitor arrives at a dead-black terminal. It boots, verifies its subsystems, and settles on the configuration screen. The highlighted rows invite a click before any explanation does. Within seconds the visitor can arm the fictional sentry, engage a contact, and hear and watch 500 rounds disappear.

That countdown is the experience. It recreates the scene’s tension with interface behavior instead of clips, stills, or exposition.

## Product framing

**UA571C.com: UA 571-C Remote Sentry System**

One sentence beneath the shared link, not on the terminal itself:

> A keyboard-controlled working-prop study of the remote sentry screen from *Aliens*.

The `.com` is intentional. SemioticStandard.org feels like an archive. UA571C.com feels like one artifact inside that archive has switched on. It is a diegetic product site where the product demonstration, operator console, and entire website are the same thing.

The screen should remain nearly context-free. Credits, commands, and rights language can live in the command overlay, repository, and page metadata so the illusion survives.

## What makes it shareable

- It is immediately recognizable to the right audience and still legible as an unusual machine to everyone else.
- The keyboard is the trick. Numbers change selectors, Space fires, and Escape cuts the system. It feels discovered rather than instructed.
- The finite 500-round state gives the visit a beginning, a climax, and an end.
- Fullscreen turns a web page into a prop without requiring installation.
- Sound is synthesized locally and only after input. There are no media assets to license, preload, or fail.

## Boundaries worth preserving

- Keep it a fan-made interface study, not an “official manual” or a real-world weapons simulation.
- Do not modernize it with glass panels, charts, gradients, or a realistic radar HUD. The crude grid and selection bars are the identity.
- Do not lead with explanation. Let the terminal boot first.
- Do not use film stills in the shipped experience. The HTML/CSS reconstruction is the artifact.
